Sound and Geometrical Optics

← Back to Home

Week 2 topics: Production and propagation of sound, Characteristics of sound, Reflection of light, Spherical mirrors, Refraction and lenses

1. Production and Propagation of Sound

Sound is produced by vibrating objects. It needs a material medium such as air, water or a solid to travel. Sound cannot travel through a vacuum. In air, sound travels as a longitudinal wave through compressions and rarefactions.

2. Characteristics of Sound

Loudness depends mainly on amplitude. Pitch depends on frequency. A high frequency sound has a high pitch. Quality or timbre helps us distinguish between sounds produced by different sources even when they have the same pitch and loudness.

The approximate range of hearing for a healthy young human is 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Frequencies below this range are called infrasonic, while frequencies above it are called ultrasonic.

3. Reflection of Light

Reflection is the bouncing back of light into the same medium after it strikes a surface. The two laws of reflection are: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.

4. Spherical Mirrors

A concave mirror has its reflecting surface curved inward. A convex mirror has its reflecting surface curved outward. Concave mirrors can form real or virtual images, depending on object position. Convex mirrors always form virtual, erect and diminished images, so they are useful as rear view mirrors.

5. Refraction and Lenses

Refraction is the bending of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium to another because its speed changes. A convex lens converges parallel rays, while a concave lens diverges them. The principal focus is the point where parallel rays meet or appear to spread from after refraction.

Remember: A ray passing through the optical centre of a thin lens travels approximately undeviated.
