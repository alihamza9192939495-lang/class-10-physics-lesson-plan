Week 3 topics: Electric charge, Charging methods, Electric field, Electric current, Potential difference and resistance, Ohm's law

1. Electric Charge

Electric charge is a basic property of matter. There are two types of charge, positive and negative. Like charges repel each other, while unlike charges attract each other. The SI unit of charge is coulomb (C).

2. Charging Methods

An object can be charged by friction, conduction or induction. Charging by friction occurs when materials are rubbed and electrons transfer from one material to another. Charging by conduction takes place through direct contact. Charging by induction occurs without direct contact.

3. Electric Field

An electric field is the region around a charge in which another electric charge experiences an electric force. Electric field lines show the direction of force on a positive test charge. They point away from positive charges and towards negative charges.

4. Electric Current

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor. Its SI unit is ampere (A). The equation is I = Q/t, where Q is charge and t is time.

5. Potential Difference and Resistance

Potential difference is the work done per unit charge to move a charge between two points. Its SI unit is volt (V). Resistance is the opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of current. Its SI unit is ohm (Ω).

6. Ohm's Law

At constant temperature and other physical conditions, the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it. The equation is V = IR.

Example: If a 6 V battery is connected across a 3 Ω resistor, current is I = V/R = 6/3 = 2 A.
