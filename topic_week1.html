Simple Harmonic Motion and Waves

← Back to Home

Week 1 topics: Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM), Characteristics of SHM, Wave motion, Types of waves, Properties of waves

1. Simple Harmonic Motion

Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) is a repeated to and fro motion in which the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement and always acts towards the mean position. Examples include the motion of a simple pendulum for a small angle and a mass attached to a spring.

Important terms

  • Mean position: The central position where the net force is zero.
  • Amplitude: The maximum displacement from the mean position.
  • Time period: Time taken to complete one vibration.
  • Frequency: Number of vibrations completed in one second. Its unit is hertz (Hz).

Frequency and time period are related by f = 1/T.

2. Characteristics of SHM

In SHM, acceleration is directed towards the mean position. It is zero at the mean position and maximum at the extreme positions. Speed is maximum at the mean position and zero at the extreme positions.

3. Wave Motion

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without the overall transfer of matter. Water waves, sound waves and light waves are common examples.

4. Types of Waves

Transverse waves: Particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave travel. Example: waves on a stretched rope.

Longitudinal waves: Particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave travel. They contain compressions and rarefactions. Sound in air is a longitudinal wave.

5. Wave Properties

Wavelength (λ) is the distance between two consecutive points in the same phase, such as crest to crest. Wave speed (v) is the distance travelled by a wave per second. The relation is v = fλ.

Remember: Amplitude is related to the energy carried by a wave. Greater amplitude means greater energy transfer.
