Week 4 topics: Magnetic effect of current, Electromagnetic induction, Basic electronic components, Diodes and transistors, Atomic and nuclear physics

1. Magnetic Effect of Current

An electric current produces a magnetic field around a conductor. The direction of the field around a straight current carrying wire can be found by the right hand grip rule: point the thumb of the right hand in the direction of current, and the curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field.

2. Electromagnetic Induction

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an induced voltage in a coil when the magnetic field linked with it changes. The induced voltage can produce current if the circuit is closed. It is used in electric generators and transformers.

3. Basic Electronic Components

A resistor limits current. A capacitor stores electric charge and energy in an electric field. A diode allows current to pass mainly in one direction. These components are used in electronic circuits.

4. Diodes and Transistors

A diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow easily in one direction and offers high resistance in the opposite direction. A transistor can be used as an electronic switch or amplifier. Transistors are important parts of computers and many electronic devices.

5. Atomic and Nuclear Physics

An atom contains a small, dense nucleus surrounded by electrons. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons. The atomic number is the number of protons, while the mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons.

Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable atomic nuclei. The three common types are alpha particles, beta particles and gamma rays. Nuclear energy can be released through fission or fusion.

Nuclear fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus into smaller nuclei. Nuclear fusion is the joining of light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus. The Sun produces energy mainly through nuclear fusion.
